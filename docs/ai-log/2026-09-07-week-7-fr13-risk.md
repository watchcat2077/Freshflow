## 1. The three rulings, applied

**Ruling 1 — ADR 0003** (`docs/decisions/0003-velocity-baseline-is-seeded-from-configuration.md`).
The config-seeded baseline is accepted as an explicit MVP simplification, on ADR 0001's argument:
the baseline enters through a port, the seeder is one legitimate implementation of that port's data
source, and a measured baseline replaces that one class. The upgrade path is recorded in four
steps, and **step 4 is called out as still undecided** — the cold-start rule for a SKU with too
little history is the same question at a smaller scale, and it will still be open when the trailing
aggregate is built. The ADR also records what must be said out loud until then: `velocityRatio` is
a ratio against an asserted normal rate, and FR13's `projectedSales` and `projectedShortfall`
inherit that caveat in full.

**Ruling 2 — spec-02 FR12.** The window-selection and staleness rule is now specified: only fully
elapsed windows are eligible, the latest of those is reported, and once even that has aged out by
more than a window length the endpoint answers 404. The spec now also states why rule 3 carries
weight: FR13 multiplies velocity by remaining trading hours, so a stale velocity projects sales
that are not happening.

**Ruling 3 — spec-01 §2.6.** Split into Table A (integration topics, in `contracts`) and Table B
(internal topics, owned by one context). The distinction is about the Published Language, not the
transport: both kinds travel through Kafka and both must carry `eventId` and `occurredAt`. What
differs is who may depend on the contract. `velocity-baseline-published` moved to Table B;
`sell-through-risk-detected` stays in Table A even though both ends are `insight` today, because
FR14's agent consumes it and `markdown-recommended` carries the conclusion to `pricing`.

## 2. FR2 was a genuine precondition

Checked first, as instructed. `stock-level-changed` had a row in §2.6 and **no producer anywhere in
the repository** — no `.to()`, no `StreamBridge`, nothing:

```
$ grep -rn "stock-level-changed\|StockLevelChanged" --include=*.java --include=*.yml .
(no matches outside spec files)
```

FR13's batch-state `KTable` needs both `batch-received` (a batch's expiry and opening quantity) and
`stock-level-changed` (everything that happens to it afterwards). Neither is sufficient alone, so
FR2 was built first.

FR2 is tagged `[POST-MVP]` in spec-02, and CLAUDE.md forbids implementing POST-MVP requirements
unprompted. It was explicitly authorised here as a precondition, which is why it was built.

### What FR2 does

| Concern | Where it lives | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| FEFO selection across batches | `FefoDeductionPolicy`, a **domain service** | spec-03 §1. The rule is about a *set* of aggregates and no single batch can see the others |
| Stock must never go negative; no deduction from expired or written-off batches | inside `StockBatch` | spec-01 §2.5 — the aggregate owns the number |
| Orchestration | `ApplyItemSoldHandler` (one command, one handler) | CLAUDE.md prefers a handler per complex use case |
| Idempotency on `eventId` | `ItemSoldMessageHandler`, the inbound adapter | Deduplicating a redelivery is a property of the delivery, not of the use case |

The policy **plans** and does not mutate: it returns a `DeductionPlan` of allocations, and each
`StockBatch` then enforces its own invariants when told to deduct its share. That split is what
keeps FEFO in the domain layer without letting a domain service reach inside aggregates.

Three decisions worth a reviewer's attention:

**A sale larger than available stock deducts what exists and reports the remainder.** The sale is a
fact that already happened upstream. Refusing it would leave remaining quantity overstated
permanently — the opposite of what FR2 exists to achieve — and throwing would stall the partition
on a message that will never succeed. It is logged as a warning, because it means this context's
stock disagrees with what the shop sold. **No specification states this**; see G-1.

**Idempotency is persisted, not in memory.** A deduction is a durable change; if the record of
having made it lived only in memory, every restart would let redelivered events deduct again and
stock would drift downward. The `eventId` row and the deduction are written in **one transaction**,
so either both happened or neither did. This is the difference from `sales-service`'s in-memory
deduplication, where re-consuming a price rule merely rewrites a snapshot with the same value.

**The `item-sold` binding uses a durable consumer group**, unlike `sales-service`'s anonymous one.
A view may be rebuilt from the whole topic by every instance; a deduction must be applied by
exactly one consumer and must not be replayed from the beginning on restart.

## 3. FR13

### 3.1 The topology

FR12 and FR13 are **one** Kafka Streams topology, because FR13 is defined as a join against "the
velocity stream" and a `KStream` cannot cross between Spring Cloud Stream functions without going
back through a topic. Four inputs and one output, expressed as a curried `Function`:

| Binding | Topic | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `insightTopology-in-0` | `item-sold` | FR12's event stream |
| `insightTopology-in-1` | `velocity-baseline-published` | FR12's baseline KTable |
| `insightTopology-in-2` | `batch-received` | batch state: expiry, opening quantity |
| `insightTopology-in-3` | `stock-level-changed` | batch state: what remains |
| `insightTopology-out-0` | `sell-through-risk-detected` | FR13's detection |

**The batch-state `KTable` is keyed by SKU, not by batch**, and holds every batch of that SKU
(`SkuBatchStates`). That is forced by the join: FR13 joins it against a **per-SKU** velocity
stream. A table keyed by batch could not be joined against a per-SKU stream at all, and a table
keyed by SKU holding one batch would silently discard all but the last. Holding the set is what
makes one velocity update assess every batch of that SKU at once.

**A stock change for a batch never seen received is dropped**, because without `batch-received`
there is no expiry, and a batch with no expiry cannot be assessed for expiry risk. Recording it
with a guessed expiry would produce a risk figure that looks authoritative and is invented.

**The velocity stream is suppressed until windows close.** Without `suppress`, every sale would
emit a dozen partial windows, each under-reporting the rate, and each would trigger a fresh risk
assessment on a figure that was never true.

**`sell-through-risk-detected` fires on the transition into risk, not on every re-assessment.** A
detection happens once; with a window advancing every five seconds, a single slow-selling batch
would otherwise emit several events a minute for as long as it stayed at risk, and the topic would
carry alarm volume rather than alarm information. The transition is computed with `reduce`, which
hands the reducer the previous assessment for that batch — exactly what is needed.

### 3.2 Where the risk rule lives

`RiskAssessmentPolicy` is in the **application** layer, not the topology. Two callers need the same
answer: the topology, which decides when to alarm, and the read side serving
`GET /api/insight/risks`. If each applied its own threshold they could disagree, and a batch could
be alarmed by one and reported safe by the other. Computing it once and storing the result makes
that impossible — and, since `insight-service` has no `domain` package, `application` is the
innermost layer available (spec-01 §2.3, §2.5). It carries no Spring annotation, so the arithmetic
is tested with `new RiskAssessmentPolicy(5.0, 24.0)` and no context at all.

### Two defects this run caught, both worth recording

**The timestamp extractor silently dropped two of the three input topics.** The first end-to-end
run produced no risk at all. `OccurredAtTimestampExtractor` still pattern-matched
`ItemSoldEvent` only, so every `batch-received` and `stock-level-changed` record returned `-1` and
Kafka Streams discarded it — the batch-state table was empty and the join had nothing to match.
The count was exact:

```
$ grep -c "dropped] topic=" insight.log
9
      1 dropped] topic=batch-received
      8 dropped] topic=stock-level-changed
```

which matched the topic offsets precisely (`batch-received:0:1`, `stock-level-changed:0:8`).

**The design that caused it also caught it.** The extractor refuses a record it cannot place rather
than falling back to ingestion time, and it says so at WARN. Had it fallen back, the topology would
have run, produced risk figures, and been *wrong* — batch state timestamped by arrival, windows
subtly misplaced, and nothing in any log to show it. This is the case §2.6's rule exists for, and
the strict reading paid for itself the first time it was exercised. Fixed by matching on
`IntegrationEvent`, the interface added this week for exactly this purpose and then not used —
which is the actual mistake, and is now recorded in the extractor's own comment.

**`sell-through-risk-detected` carried a non-conforming `occurredAt`.** It serialised as
`1788762590.000000000` where spec-01 §2.6 requires an ISO-8601 timestamp and every other topic
carries one. Cause: the Kafka Streams output serde uses Spring Kafka's own `ObjectMapper`, which
writes `Instant` as an epoch decimal; the other topics are produced through the message-channel
binder, which uses Spring Boot's mapper and already writes ISO-8601. Fixed in
`SellThroughRiskDetectedEventSerde` and re-verified above. **A consumer written against the other
topics would not have parsed it** — and no test would have caught it, because no test serialises
this topic.

## 5. What I assumed that the specs did not state

| # | Assumption | Why, and what it would cost to change |
|---|---|---|
| A | A sale exceeding available stock deducts what exists and logs the remainder | FR2 says only "stock must never become negative". The sale already happened; refusing it would overstate stock permanently and throwing would stall the partition. See G-1 |
| B | The consumed-`eventId` record is persisted in the service's own H2 and never pruned | Idempotency must survive restart to be worth anything. The table grows without bound; at this volume that is acceptable, and pruning needs a retention rule nobody has set |
| C | Batch state is keyed by SKU, holding all batches of that SKU | Forced by FR13's own wording: the join partner is a per-SKU velocity stream |
| D | A `stock-level-changed` for an unknown batch is dropped | No expiry, so no expiry risk. Better than inventing one |
| E | `shortfall-threshold` 5.0 units | FR13 gives no threshold. Zero would alarm on missing by one unit. See G-2 |
| F | `trading-hours-per-day` 24.0 | FR13 says "remaining **trading** hours" and nothing defines the trading day. See G-3 |
| G | `hoursToExpiry` is whole hours, truncated | spec-04 types it `integer`. Truncation means a batch with 119 minutes left reports 1 |
| H | Risk fires on the transition into risk only | "Detected" is an event, not a state. The standing list is the endpoint |
| I | An expired or depleted batch is never flagged | FR13 exists to catch batches "early enough that a markdown can still change the outcome"; an expired batch is FR3's write-off |
| J | Assessment happens when a velocity window closes, so a SKU with no sales is never re-assessed | See G-4 — this is the significant one |

## 6. Deviations from the specs

**D1 — FR2 is `[POST-MVP]` and was built anyway.** Explicitly authorised as a precondition for
FR13. Recorded because CLAUDE.md forbids it unprompted.

**D2 — `BatchRiskView` carries an `atRisk` field spec-03 §4 does not list.** The topology assesses
every batch it sees, not only the alarming ones, and the flag separates them. It is not serialised:
`RiskResponseDto` omits it, because every element of that response is by definition currently
flagged.

**D3 — the topology, the serdes, the extractor and the join have no automated test.** Unchanged
from Week 7 part 1, and it cost more this time: both defects in §4 were found by running the
system, not by the suite. `TopologyTestDriver` would have caught the extractor bug in seconds.
**The request for `kafka-streams-test-utils` (test scope) is now the most valuable outstanding
dependency ask**, ahead of `spring-cloud-stream-test-binder`.

**D4 — `contracts` gained an `IntegrationEvent` interface** that no specification mentions. It
states §2.6's "every event MUST carry `eventId` and `occurredAt`" in the type system. Reported
because it changes the shape of the Published Language module.

**D5 — the demonstration compresses time heavily.** A 60-second window standing in for 60 minutes,
and 400 units with two hours of shelf life. The arithmetic is unchanged; the scenario is not
realistic retail. Worth saying plainly on Monday rather than being asked.

## 7. Where I judge the specs incomplete — NOT acted on

**G-1. FR2 does not say what happens when a sale exceeds available stock.** "Stock on hand must
never become negative" constrains the outcome without choosing between deducting what exists,
rejecting the message, or quarantining it. The implementation deducts and warns. This is a real
operational question — it means the shop sold something this system did not know it had — and it
deserves a decision rather than a default.

**G-2. FR13 gives no shortfall threshold.** It says to compare projected sales against remaining
quantity but not how large a gap is worth reporting. The number decides how noisy the system is
and is a merchandising judgement, not an engineering one.

**G-3. "Remaining trading hours" is undefined.** No specification describes the trading day. At 24
hours a day the projection is over clock hours, which will overstate what a batch can sell. A shop
open twelve hours sells half as much before expiry as the clock suggests, so this is not a cosmetic
default — it changes which batches are reported.

**G-4. Nothing says when risk should be re-assessed, and the streaming answer has a real hole.**
Assessment is triggered by a closing velocity window, which requires sales. **A SKU that stops
selling entirely stops being assessed**, so a batch that becomes at risk *because* sales stopped
may never be flagged — precisely the case FR13's user story describes ("a slow day or a genuine
problem"). Batches already flagged stay flagged and stay listed, so the endpoint does not go
stale; but no new alarm fires. Fixing it means a periodic trigger — a punctuator, or a wall-clock
tick joined into the topology — which is a design decision with its own event-time complications,
and it is not Week 7 part 2's scope. **Recommend this be the first item of Week 8.**

**G-5. spec-03 §4's `BatchRiskView` has no field for whether the batch is at risk**, though FR13's
whole purpose is that determination. See D2.

**G-6. §2.6's "every event MUST carry `occurredAt` (ISO-8601)" is not enforced anywhere.** The
serde defect in §4 produced a conforming *value* in a non-conforming *format*, and nothing failed.
A contract test over the `contracts` module — serialise each event, assert the shape — would catch
it and would need no broker. Recommend it for Week 8; it is cheap and it protects every consumer.
